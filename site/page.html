Student Life

What students do outside class

Which activities students choose, how that is shifting, which organizations they join, stay in and lead, and where it leads. From the team's student-experience notebook.

Activities

{{CELL:life_experience_share|pctCur.sort_values().plot.barh()}}

Student organizations are the most common experience (66% of current students), then internships (44%) and campus jobs (42%). Co-ops are the rarest at 8%.

{{/CELL}} {{CELL:life_breadth|stuExp.groupby("campus_id")["experience_type"].nunique()}}

Most students try 2 to 4 different types of experience. 181 people have tried none, and nobody has tried all ten.

{{/CELL}}
{{CELL:life_type_change|typeShare["2024–26"] - typeShare["2018–20"]}}

Certifications gained the most ground (+1.8 pts) and internships lost the most (−4.3 pts). These are shares, not raw counts, because current students make recent years busier for every type.

{{/CELL}} {{CELL:life_share_by_year|shareByYear = counts.div(counts.sum(axis=1), axis=0)}}

Internships peaked at 27% of all participation in 2021 and fell to 17% by 2026, while certifications jumped to 21% and overtook them.

{{/CELL}}

Organizations

Strategies

Which in-school choices pay off in job hunting

Alumni only: they have both campus records and job outcomes. Salary premium = first-job salary minus the median of grads with the same major and graduation year. From the team's strategies notebook.

Internships and job routes

{{CELL:time_to_hire|a.plot_time_to_hire(intern["df_intern"])}}

Most employed alumni with 2 or 3 internships were hired within a month of graduation, while those with 0 or 1 took much longer. A 3rd internship adds little over a 2nd for both salary and hire time.

{{/CELL}}
{{CELL:strat_channels|stuAlum.groupby("first_job_found_via")["sal_adj"].median()}}

Only return offers pay above similar grads (+$7.2K median). Every other route lands $1K to $4K below.

{{/CELL}} {{CELL:life_return_offer|offerRate[offerRate["size"] >= 30].nlargest(10, "mean")}}

Copper Ridge Data Centers turns 52% of internships and co-ops into return offers, against 41% across all companies.

{{/CELL}}

What to do on campus

{{CELL:strat_activity_payoff|stats.mannwhitneyu(withSalary.loc[did, "sal_adj"], withSalary.loc[~did, "sal_adj"])}}

Internships are both the most common (72% of alumni) and the biggest payoff (+$10.4K). Certifications, co-ops and research add about $4.5K to $5K. Hackathons, campus jobs and competitive teams show no significant gap.

{{/CELL}}
{{CELL:strat_clubs|stuAlum.assign(role=role).groupby("role")[["sal_adj", "months_to_first_job"]].median()}}

Raw numbers favor leaders (+$2.5K and 0.5 months to a first job, against −$1.6K and 1.6 months with no club). Once internships, GPA, experience types and credentials are held constant, being a leader adds nothing (see the regression below).

{{/CELL}} {{CELL:life_salary_premium|orgRank[orgRank["n"] >= 30].nlargest(10, "premium")}}

Three cybersecurity certification bodies lead, each about $10K above similar grads, followed by consulting and software internship employers.

{{/CELL}}
{{CELL:life_breadth_salary|ax.boxplot([adj[grp == k] for k in range(11)])}}

The median first-job salary rises with the number of experience types, from about $12K below similar grads with none to about $7K above with six. The groups with 8 or more are tiny (5, 1 and 0 alumni).

{{/CELL}}
{{CELL:strat_gpa|ax.hexbin(withSalary["final_gpa"], withSalary["sal_adj"], gridsize=25, mincnt=1)}}

Starting pay climbs steadily with GPA: the median goes from $19K below similar grads at a 1.75–2.0 GPA to $15K above at 3.75–4.0. GPA is the strongest factor in the regression (+$13.2K per GPA point).

{{/CELL}}
Statistical checks behind each chart title

One factor at a time: Spearman for ordered factors, Mann-Whitney for two-group gaps (salaries are skewed, so no t-tests).

{{T:strategy_checks}}
Which factors still matter together (regression)

OLS regressions for alumni with a first job. Each number is the change for one more unit of that factor, holding the others constant. Club leadership drops out: its raw gap came from leaders also interning more and having higher GPAs.

Job Trajectory

How grads find their first job, and what happens next

Alumni only. First-job facts come from the alumni file; everything after the first job comes from their employment history. From the team's job-hunting notebook.

Landing the first job

{{CELL:job_destinations|pd.crosstab(responded["graduation_year"], responded["first_destination"].map(groups), normalize="index")}}

Every year, most grads are employed full-time about six months out (69% to 83%). 2020 was the hardest year: 64% full-time and 16% still seeking.

{{/CELL}}
{{CELL:job_time_to_hire|np.sort(hired["months_to_first_job"].dropna())}}

Half of hired alumni start within 0.9 months, three quarters within 3.2 months and 90% within 4.8 months. 18% start right at graduation.

{{/CELL}} {{CELL:job_channels|hired.groupby("first_job_found_via")["months_to_first_job"].agg(["size", "median"])}}

Return offers are the most common route (29% of first jobs) and by far the fastest, starting a median 0.2 months after graduation. Every other route takes about two months.

{{/CELL}}
{{CELL:job_industries|hired["first_employer_industry"].value_counts(normalize=True)}}

46% of first jobs are in the federal ecosystem: federal contracting (22%), federal government (12%), and defense & aerospace (11%).

{{/CELL}}

Pay and moving up

{{CELL:job_regions|firstJobs.groupby("region")[["annual_salary_usd", "adjusted"]].median()}}

On paper, DC and Northern Virginia pay the most. After cost of living, remote jobs pay the most ($83.1K), and Baltimore ($73.8K) edges out DC ($72.1K).

{{/CELL}} {{CELL:job_raises|raisePct.groupby(jobs["change_type"]).agg(["median", "size"])}}

Moving up pays: a new employer at a higher level brings a median 21% raise and an internal promotion 18%. Lateral moves bring about 4%.

{{/CELL}}
{{CELL:job_ladder|pd.crosstab(yearsIn, level, normalize="index")}}

Five years after their first job, 38% of the roles alumni start are Senior or higher, up from almost none in the first two years.

{{/CELL}}
Statistical checks behind each chart title

Chi-square for rates, Mann-Whitney for skewed money and time gaps. The four Baltimore comparisons are Bonferroni-corrected (p × 4). Only supported claims appear in the titles and captions.

Majors and their effect on success

{{CELL:salary_by_major_degree|a.plot_salary_by_major_degree(majors["alumcareerinfo"])}}

Most alumni in the dataset only hold a bachelor's degree (2,032 vs 115 with a master's), so the master's comparisons are built on small samples. The typical salary gap between Computer Science and Information Systems is small: CS alumni earn a median of about 3% more, and the two distributions overlap almost entirely.

{{/CELL}}
{{CELL:salary_trend|a.plot_salary_trend(majors["trend"])}}

IS majors close the initial gap by around the 5-year mark. Fewer alumni remain beyond year 8, so the gap at the end of the chart is a less certain result.

Internships and their effect on success

{{CELL:salary_by_internships|a.plot_salary_by_internships(intern["df_intern"])}}

There is a large difference in starting salary between alumni with 0 and 3 internships, but the 2- and 3-internship distributions overlap heavily. Salary gains level off after 2 internships.

{{/CELL}}
{{CELL:strat_internships|alum.groupby("nIntern")[["months_to_first_job", "sal_adj"]].median()}}

Internships help on every count: full-time employment rises from 70% with none to 78% with two or more, time to the first job drops from 4.2 to 0.3 months, and pay goes from $10.0K below similar grads to $8.5K above with three or more.

{{/CELL}}
{{CELL:return_offers|a.plot_return_offers(intern["offer_table"])}}

At every internship count, alumni whose first job was a return offer earned a higher median starting salary: +$5,250 with 1 internship and +$6,750 with 2 or 3. Return offers also become much more common with more internships (25% with 1, 47% with 2, 66% with 3), so part of the value of more internships is a better chance of converting one into a higher-paying offer.

Student activities

{{CELL:experience_share|a.plot_experience_share(activities["pct_curr"])}}

Student organizations are by far the most common experience (66%), followed by internships (44%) and campus jobs (42%). Since internships are tied to higher salaries, faster hiring, and lower unemployment, over half of current students not having one yet is a clear opportunity, though some of this is expected since about half are freshmen or sophomores.

{{/CELL}}

3 of the top 4 are certification providers, and most of the rest are internship employers in industries that also paid well as a first job, like Cloud & Infrastructure, Telecommunications, and Cybersecurity Services. This shows correlation, not causation: students who pursue these may already be heading toward higher-paying fields.

Method

How we work

Every finding goes through the same five steps. The analysis code lives in analysis.py and the dashboard in app.py.

  1. 01

    Load

    Six CSV files from the hackUMBC 2026 dataset, joined on each person's campus ID.

    pandas · read_csv
  2. 02

    Clean

    Turn "Not Applicable" into missing values, convert salaries to numbers, count repeat activities once per person.

    pandas
  3. 03

    Explore

    Box plots, hiring curves, and salary trends, one notebook cell per question.

    Jupyter · matplotlib
  4. 04

    Measure

    Variance explained by each factor, and salary compared with peers who have the same years of experience.

    η² (ANOVA) · numpy
  5. 05

    Share

    Package every chart as a function and serve them in an interactive dashboard.

    Streamlit
